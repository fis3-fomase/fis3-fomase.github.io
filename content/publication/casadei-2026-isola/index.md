abstract: 'Emerging paradigms such as the Internet of Things (IoT), Cyber-Physical
  Systems, and Wireless Sensor Networks envision distributed computing platforms as
  dense deployments of interacting devices embedded within physical environments (e.g.,
  agricultural fields, smart cities, traffic systems). To effectively engineer these
  systems, we must shift our focus from individual devices to the collective behaviour
  of the ensemble, designing distributed algorithms from a global perspective. Many
  of these algorithms produce a collective result—a computational field of values
  distributed across space and time—that converges as device density and execution
  speed approach infinity. Consequently, the behaviour of such algorithms can be formally
  captured by computational fields defined over continuous domains: deployment on
  a discrete network simply leads to an approximation over the ideal continuous behaviour.
  In this paper, we define a formal framework to capture this idea, and accordingly
  characterise some fundamental algorithms of collective adaptive behaviour.'
